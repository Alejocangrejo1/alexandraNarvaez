What We Did

Planning, monitoring and adjusting

Plan
Monitor
Adjust

In this part of the course, we worked on Barry Zimmerman’s Self-Regulated Learning (SRL) model based on his article Becoming a Self-Regulated Learner. Instead of just reading about the theory, we applied it during a speaking activity centered on Green Day’s song Wake Me Up When September Ends. In class, we used a set of guiding question cards split into five parts: forethought (before speaking), performance (during speaking), self-reflection (after speaking), adaptation (for next time), and connecting our Language Learning Strategies (LLS) with SRL.

Before starting the task, I had to set a specific goal and pick the strategies I wanted to use. My goal was to talk about the song without stopping to translate every word in my head, relying on synonyms or paraphrasing whenever I forgot a word. While I was speaking, keeping the performance questions in mind helped me notice right away when I got nervous or paused too much, so I was able to slow down and rephrase my ideas on the spot instead of getting stuck.

Going through the self-reflection and adaptation cards at the end showed me how much smoother a speaking task goes when you actually plan ahead. Normally, I just start talking and hope things come out right, so breaking the task down into planning, monitoring, and adjusting gave me way more control over what I was saying. As a future teacher, I found this exercise super useful because these simple guiding questions can help students feel less anxious and take charge of their own speaking practice.

The Five Parts

One question card for every moment

  1. 1

  2. 2

  3. 3

    Self-reflection

    After speaking: judge what worked and why.

  4. 4

    Adaptation

    Next time: keep, change and set a new goal.
